# Measuring AI Value: Beyond Time Saved

**THE PRACTICAL AI SERIES · MEASUREMENT**

An AI workflow can look impressive in a demo and still fail to create business value. Faster output is only one part of the equation. Measure whether the process became cheaper, more reliable or more useful — without shifting hidden work elsewhere.

## Start with a baseline

Before a pilot, record how the process works today. Choose a representative task and measure volume, active staff time, completion time, quality and exceptions. Use the same definitions and comparable work after introducing AI. Otherwise, a change in workload or case complexity can be mistaken for an AI improvement.

### A simple measurement cycle

#### BASELINE

Measure the current process.

#### PILOT

Test with human review.

#### COMPARE

Check quality, cost and outcomes.

## Measure five things

- **Throughput:** how much work is completed in a fixed period?

- **Cycle time:** how long from request to completion?

- **Human effort:** include checking, correction and escalation — not just the AI's processing time.

- **Quality:** how often is the result accurate and usable first time?

- **Exceptions and risk:** how often does a person intervene, and are privacy and approval controls still working?

## A simple example

Imagine a fictional team classifies 1,200 service requests each month. Manual handling takes four active minutes per request. An AI-assisted process, including human checking, takes 2.5 minutes.

The difference is 1.5 minutes per request, or 30 hours of theoretical staff capacity each month. At an illustrative loaded labour cost of €30 per hour, that capacity has a notional value of €900.

Now assume software costs €90 per month, setup amortisation €150, monitoring €60 and quality checks/rework €90. Total additional monthly cost is €390. The simplified net capacity value is therefore €510.

**But €510 is not automatically a cash saving.** If payroll stays the same, the team has gained capacity, not reduced expenditure. The business must show how that time is used — for example, clearing a backlog, improving service or handling growth without adding shifts. These figures are illustrative, not a forecast.

## Do not trade quality for speed

A faster workflow can create more work downstream if it sends cases to the wrong team. During a pilot, sample outputs and track incorrect categories, missed urgent cases, staff overrides and rework. Keep human approval where an error could materially affect a customer, employee or financial transaction.

## Make the decision

- **Scale carefully** when quality is acceptable, controls work and the benefit is evidenced.

- **Revise and retest** when a fixable issue — poor data, high review effort or inconsistent output — limits value.

- **Stop** when the workflow adds cost or risk without enough measurable benefit.

**The value test:** measure the business improvement, subtract the full cost of running and controlling the process, and keep quality within agreed limits. If the only evidence is that AI produced an answer quickly, the business case is not finished.

*Example figures are synthetic and intended to demonstrate the calculation. They are not actual client results.*
