# Small Business AI on a Budget: Count the Whole Cost

An AI subscription can look cheap. The real cost also includes setup, integration, staff time, checking, maintenance and mistakes. A sensible decision compares the whole process — not just the monthly licence.

## Start with one specific task

Choose a measurable job, such as sorting routine enquiries or extracting fields from a standard document. Record how often it happens, how long it takes, who does it and what a correct result looks like. “Use more AI” is not a business objective.

### Count the full cost

#### SOFTWARE

Licence and usage

#### BUILD

Setup and integration

#### OPERATE

People and review

#### CONTROL

Risk and maintenance

## Include the hidden work

- **Software:** subscriptions, usage charges, add-ons and minimum commitments.

- **Setup and integration:** prompts, rules, permissions, testing and connections to existing systems.

- **People:** training, process ownership, support, human review and exception handling.

- **Quality:** checking outputs, correcting errors and reworking failed cases.

- **Risk and change:** access controls, logging, data protection review, fallback procedures and updates when systems change.

## Estimate the time released

Imagine a fictional team handling 200 routine cases each month. The current process takes eight minutes per case. An assisted process might take five minutes plus one minute of human review. That releases 400 minutes a month before setup, maintenance and exceptions are counted.

Released time is not automatically a cash saving. Ask whether it can reduce overtime, clear a backlog, improve response times or support more work without extra shifts. If the time is not put to useful work, its financial value may be limited.

## Run a small, reversible pilot

Test a low-risk workflow with synthetic or approved data. Keep a person responsible for checking results. Measure total handling time, correction rate, exceptions, failures and all tool and staff costs. Agree in advance what would justify continuing — and what would make you stop.

## Sometimes AI is the wrong tool

For stable, structured tasks, a rule, spreadsheet formula, form validation or ordinary integration may be cheaper and more predictable. Sometimes the best improvement is removing an unnecessary step or fixing poor source data. AI is worth testing when interpretation of variable information adds value and the output can be checked.

**The practical test:** compare the full cost of the proposed solution with the current process. If the evidence is weak, keep the experiment small — or do not automate.
